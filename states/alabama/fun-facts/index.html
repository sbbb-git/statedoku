ALAlabama Fun Facts

Capital: Montgomery · South · Admitted 1819

Alabama has more species of freshwater fish, snails, mussels, crayfish and turtles than any other state, and the gap is enormous. The Mobile River basin drains most of the state into the Gulf and has been running in isolation long enough to produce hundreds of animals that live nowhere else on Earth. Biologists rank it as the richest temperate freshwater system in North America, which is the one thing about Alabama that almost never reaches a trivia list.

Facts most people get wrong

  • Heart of Dixie is not the official nickname. The official one is the Yellowhammer State, from an 1861 cavalry company out of Huntsville whose uniforms were trimmed in bright yellow cloth. Heart of Dixie was a slogan the state chamber of commerce put on license plates in the 1950s to sell tourism.
  • New Orleans did not invent American Mardi Gras. Mobile was founded in 1702, sixteen years before New Orleans existed, and kept Carnival under French rule. The first mystic parading society, the Cowbellion de Rakin Society, formed in Mobile in 1830, decades before the first New Orleans krewe paraded in 1857.
  • Birmingham is not the largest city anymore. Huntsville passed it in the 2020 census. Montgomery has been the capital since 1846. Three different cities hold the three titles outsiders usually pin on one.
  • Alabama does not grow half the nation's peanuts. Georgia leads the country. The number comes from Dothan in the southeast corner, where roughly half the US crop grows inside a radius that spills over into Georgia and Florida.
  • The boll weevil monument in Enterprise is sincere. Put up in 1919, it thanks the beetle that destroyed the cotton crop and pushed farmers into peanuts, which paid better. It remains the only monument anywhere built to honor an agricultural pest.
  • The Iron Bowl is not named for iron will. Alabama and Auburn met at Birmingham's Legion Field, in the middle of the iron and steel district, from 1948 until the game began rotating to campus stadiums in 1989.

Records and firsts

  • Montgomery opened the first citywide electric streetcar system in the United States, the Lightning Route, in April 1886.
  • The Saturn V that carried Apollo 11 to the Moon was developed at Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, which opened in 1960.
  • The 54 mile march from Selma to Montgomery in March 1965 produced the Voting Rights Act, signed that August.
  • Birmingham's Vulcan is the largest cast iron statue in the world at 56 feet, cast for the 1904 St. Louis World's Fair.
  • Alabama's 1901 constitution accumulated more than 900 amendments, the longest operative constitution on record, until voters approved a recompiled version in 2022.
  • Helen Keller, born in Tuscumbia in 1880, became the first deafblind person to earn a bachelor of arts degree, from Radcliffe in 1904.

Geography that surprises

  • Alabama has more than 1,300 miles of navigable inland waterways, more than any other state. Barges reach Birmingham, Montgomery and Decatur.
  • The Gulf coastline runs only about 50 miles, among the shortest of any coastal state, yet the Port of Mobile serves the entire Tennessee Valley through the Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway.
  • Cheaha Mountain tops out at 2,413 feet and marks the southern tail of the Appalachians. South of it the state flattens into coastal plain all the way to the water.
  • The whole state runs on Central time, so crossing the Chattahoochee River east into Georgia costs you an hour.
  • Alabama sits in Dixie Alley, where a far larger share of tornadoes hit after dark than on the Great Plains. That is the main reason its tornado death tolls run high.

Where the name comes from

Alabama takes its name from the Alabama or Alibamu people, a Muskogean speaking nation who lived along the river that carries the same name. Chroniclers traveling with Hernando de Soto recorded it as Alibamo in 1540, which makes it one of the oldest written place names in the interior South. Congress applied it to the Alabama Territory in 1817, split off from Mississippi, and statehood followed in 1819.

The translation printed in old schoolbooks, thicket clearers or vegetation gatherers from the Choctaw words albah and amo, traces back to a newspaper piece by the Mobile writer Alexander Beaufort Meek in the 1850s. Linguists have never been able to confirm it. The real meaning of the tribal name is simply not known, and the honest answer is that Alabama is named after a people, not after a phrase.

Symbols in brief: bird, the yellowhammer, adopted 1927. Flower, the camellia, 1959. Tree, the longleaf pine, 1997. Motto, Audemus jura nostra defendere, Latin for we dare defend our rights.

Frequently asked questions

What is Alabama known for?

Rockets, civil rights history, college football and freshwater biodiversity. Huntsville developed the Saturn V that flew Apollo 11, Montgomery and Selma anchor the civil rights trail, the Alabama and Auburn Iron Bowl is the most intense in-state rivalry in college sports, and the Mobile River basin holds more freshwater fish, snail and mussel species than any other state.

What is the weirdest law in Alabama?

Bear wrestling. Alabama Code section 13A-12-5 makes it a crime to promote, take part in or profit from a bear wrestling exhibition, and the penalties reach the venue owner as well as the promoter. The statute exists because bear wrestling was a real roadside attraction in the state.

Why is Alabama called the Yellowhammer State?

In 1861 a company of Confederate cavalry from Huntsville arrived in camp wearing new uniforms trimmed with bright yellow cloth at the collar, cuffs and coattails. Soldiers already there shouted yellowhammer at them, after the flash of gold under a northern flicker's wing. The bird became the official state bird in 1927 and the nickname spread to the whole state.

What is the oldest city in Alabama?

Mobile, founded by the French in 1702 as Fort Louis de la Louisiane and the capital of French Louisiana until 1720. It is sixteen years older than New Orleans and has flown French, British, Spanish, Confederate and United States flags.

What food is Alabama famous for?

White barbecue sauce, a mayonnaise and vinegar sauce invented by Bob Gibson at his Decatur pit in 1925 and served over smoked chicken. Alabama is also known for fried green tomatoes, boiled peanuts from the Dothan peanut belt, and Gulf shrimp and oysters landed at Bayou La Batre.
