TNTennessee Geography

Capital: Nashville · South · Admitted 1796

A state 440 miles wide and barely 120 deep

Measure Tennessee from the Mississippi River to the Appalachian crest and you get about 440 miles. Measure it from the Kentucky line to the Alabama line and you get about 120. That long thin rectangle touches eight other states, a total matched only by Missouri: Kentucky, Virginia, North Carolina, Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, Arkansas and Missouri itself. Driving Interstate 40 across it takes you from river silt at 200 feet near Memphis to spruce and fir above 6,000 feet on the North Carolina border, a climb that in most of the country would require crossing two or three states.

The eastern end tops out at Clingmans Dome, 6,643 feet, which the United States Board on Geographic Names restored to its Cherokee name Kuwohi in 2024. It is the highest summit in Tennessee and the highest point on the entire Appalachian Trail. The floor of the state is the Mississippi River where it crosses into the state of Mississippi, at 178 feet.

The Grand Divisions are law, not habit

East, Middle and West Tennessee are not regional nicknames. They are legal entities. The state constitution forbids more than two of the five Supreme Court justices coming from any one division, and the court sits in three cities as a result, Knoxville for the east, Nashville for the middle and Jackson for the west. The three stars on the state flag stand for the same three units. The divisions follow terrain rather than politics: the Tennessee River's two crossings mark off the west, and the Cumberland Plateau escarpment walls off the east.

Climbing the ladder from river mud to spruce

Almost no state of this size crosses so many physiographic provinces in order. From the western edge they run: the Mississippi alluvial plain, then the Gulf Coastal Plain of West Tennessee, then the Western Valley where the Tennessee River flows north, then the Highland Rim, then the Central Basin, then the Cumberland Plateau, then the Ridge and Valley, and finally the Unaka and Blue Ridge mountains along the North Carolina line.

The Central Basin around Nashville looks like a bowl but began as a bulge. The Nashville Dome arched upward, the strained rock at its crest fractured, and erosion then hollowed out the top faster than the flanks, leaving a lowland 300 to 500 feet below the Highland Rim that surrounds it. The limestone underneath that whole middle section dissolves readily, which is why the Tennessee Cave Survey has catalogued more than ten thousand caves, more than any other state.

Temperature extremes: 113 degrees at Perryville on 9 August 1930, and 32 below zero at Mountain City on 30 December 1917. The Smokies receive over 80 inches of precipitation a year on the high ridges, while the Ridge and Valley floor gets closer to 45.

A river that leaves the state and comes back

The Tennessee River starts at Knoxville, where the Holston and the French Broad meet. It runs southwest to Chattanooga, saws through Walden Ridge in the Tennessee River Gorge, then crosses into Alabama, loops west along the northern edge of that state, clips the corner of Mississippi and turns north. It re-enters Tennessee near Pickwick and runs straight up the western part of the state to leave for Kentucky and, eventually, the Ohio at Paducah. The state's namesake river therefore crosses it twice in opposite directions, and those two crossings are what bracket Middle Tennessee. The Tennessee Valley Authority, created in 1933, turned nearly all of it into a staircase of reservoirs, beginning with Norris Dam in 1936.

Reelfoot Lake in the northwest corner has a different origin. During the New Madrid earthquakes of the winter of 1811 and 1812 the ground there subsided and the Mississippi backfilled the hollow. It is the largest natural lake in the state, and drowned bald cypress still stand in it two centuries later.

A northern border drawn by a bad survey

The Kentucky boundary was meant to follow 36 degrees 30 minutes north. The surveyors sent out in 1779 and 1780 could not hold the parallel, later crews corrected in fits, and the line as it stands wanders north and south of the intended latitude by several miles with visible jogs where one survey handed off to the next. The western boundary is stranger still. Just above Tiptonville the Mississippi loops almost back on itself and encloses the Kentucky Bend, a piece of Kentucky reachable by road only through Tennessee and bordered on its other sides by Missouri. Because the river has since cut new channels, the legal state line no longer sits in the water in several places.

Tennessee also splits between two time zones. East Tennessee keeps Eastern Time and the rest of the state keeps Central, with the boundary following county lines near the western edge of the Cumberland Plateau. Chattanooga and Knoxville therefore run an hour ahead of Nashville, which is roughly 110 miles from the line.
