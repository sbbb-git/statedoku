MIMichigan History

Lansing · Great Lakes · Admitted January 26, 1837, the 26th state

Michigan gave up Toledo and was handed the Upper Peninsula

Through 1835 and into 1836, Ohio and the Territory of Michigan marched militia toward a wedge of land at the western end of Lake Erie that each claimed, because the Northwest Ordinance drew the line one way and Ohio's constitution described it another. No one died in the Toledo War. A deputy sheriff was stabbed with a penknife. Congress ended it by awarding Ohio the Toledo Strip and giving Michigan the western Upper Peninsula as compensation, land nobody in Detroit particularly wanted. The convention accepted under protest in December 1836 and Michigan was admitted on January 26, 1837. Within fifteen years the consolation prize was shipping copper off the Keweenaw and iron out of the Marquette Range, and Toledo looked like a bargain.

The Council of Three Fires

The Anishinaabe held the peninsulas long before any of that. The Odawa, Ojibwe and Potawatomi, allied as the Council of Three Fires, controlled respectively the straits and the northern shores, Lake Superior, and the southwest toward the Saint Joseph River. Wyandot villages sat near the mouth of the Detroit River, while Menominee, Miami and Sauk territory touched the edges. People had been cutting native copper out of Isle Royale and the Keweenaw for several thousand years and trading it down the Mississippi, long enough that pits and hammerstones were still visible when nineteenth century prospectors arrived and simply followed them.

Fort Pontchartrain and three sovereigns

Jacques Marquette founded a mission at Sault Sainte Marie in 1668, making it the oldest continuously occupied town in the state, and another at Saint Ignace in 1671. Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac planted Fort Pontchartrain du Détroit on the narrows of the river on July 24, 1701, specifically to keep English traders out of the upper lakes. France lost the whole interior in 1763, and the Odawa leader Pontiac besieged Detroit for five months that summer in a rising that ran across the Great Lakes. Britain then kept Detroit and Mackinac for thirteen years after the 1783 peace treaty said otherwise, surrendering them only in 1796 under the Jay Treaty.

1805 Michigan Territory created; Detroit burns to the ground in June before the new governor arrives, which is why the seal reads that we hope for better things and it shall rise from the ashes.

1812 General William Hull surrenders Detroit to a smaller British force on August 16 without firing.

1813 Oliver Hazard Perry takes Lake Erie in September, and the territory is recovered.

1825 The Erie Canal opens and settlers from New York and New England pour through Detroit.

Copper, iron, pine, and a party started in a field

Michigan abolished capital punishment for murder in 1846, the first English-speaking jurisdiction anywhere to do so, and has never brought it back. Two years earlier the surveyor William Burt had watched his compass needle swing uselessly near Negaunee, which is how the Marquette iron range was found; the Soo Locks, finished in 1855, made it possible to move that ore past the rapids at Sault Sainte Marie and down to the furnaces. Keweenaw copper mines drew Cornish, Finnish and Italian labor into a district that stayed the country's leading producer until the 1880s. White pine logging stripped the lower peninsula between the 1860s and the 1890s, and Michigan lumber rebuilt Chicago after its fire. On July 6, 1854, antislavery Whigs, Free Soilers and Democrats angry about the Kansas-Nebraska Act met outdoors at Jackson because the hall could not hold them, and organized as the Republican Party.

The industry that reorganized the country

Ransom Olds was building cars in Detroit by 1899. Henry Ford incorporated the Ford Motor Company on June 16, 1903, ran a moving assembly line at Highland Park from 1913, and announced the five dollar day on January 5, 1914, which pulled workers out of the American South, Appalachia, Poland and Lebanon. William Durant assembled General Motors at Flint on September 16, 1908. Detroit's Black population went from roughly 6,000 in 1910 to more than 120,000 by 1930, into a housing market fenced off by restrictive covenants. Sit-down strikers held GM's Fisher Body plants at Flint from December 30, 1936 until February 11, 1937 and forced the corporation to recognize the United Auto Workers, the decisive victory of American industrial unionism. Willow Run was turning out a B-24 bomber roughly every hour by 1944.

What came after the boom

Freeway construction through Black Bottom and Paradise Valley, redlined mortgages and a police force at war with the neighborhoods it patrolled produced the uprising of July 23 to 27, 1967, which left 43 people dead and accelerated a population loss Detroit has never reversed. Coleman Young was elected the city's first Black mayor in 1973. The Mackinac Bridge, opened on November 1, 1957, finally tied the two peninsulas together after 120 years of ferries. Detroit filed the largest municipal bankruptcy in American history on July 18, 2013 and emerged from it in December 2014. Flint, switched to Flint River water in April 2014 without corrosion control, spent years with lead coming out of its taps.

Questions people ask about Michigan history

When did Michigan become a state?

January 26, 1837, as the 26th state, after Congress required it to drop its claim to the Toledo Strip.

What was the Toledo War?

A boundary dispute with Ohio in 1835 and 1836. Militia mobilized on both sides but no one was killed in combat.

Why does Michigan own the Upper Peninsula?

Congress transferred it as compensation for the Toledo Strip in 1836. Copper on the Keweenaw and iron on the Marquette, Menominee and Gogebic ranges made it far more valuable than the land Michigan surrendered.

Who lived in Michigan before European contact?

The Odawa, Ojibwe and Potawatomi, allied as the Council of Three Fires, with Wyandot, Menominee, Miami and Sauk communities alongside them.

When did the auto industry start in Michigan?

Ransom Olds was producing in Detroit in 1899, Ford Motor Company was incorporated in 1903, and General Motors was formed at Flint in 1908. The Highland Park moving assembly line dates from 1913.
