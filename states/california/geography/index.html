CACalifornia Geography

Capital: Sacramento · West · Admitted 1850

Mount Whitney tops out at 14,505 feet. Badwater Basin sits 282 feet below sea level. The two are about 85 miles apart, and both fall inside Inyo County, with Whitney's summit resting on the county's western line. That single county therefore contains the highest and the lowest ground in the contiguous United States, a spread of nearly 14,800 feet. No other state comes near it.

The western edge is sliding north

California is one of the few places on land where two tectonic plates grind past each other rather than one diving under the other. The San Andreas Fault runs roughly 800 miles from the Salton Sea north to Cape Mendocino, and along it the Pacific plate moves northwest against the North American plate at about 46 millimetres a year, roughly two inches. Los Angeles rides the Pacific side. San Francisco sits on the North American side. Given enough millions of years, one arrives at the latitude of the other.

The consequences are on the record. The April 18, 1906 earthquake ruptured close to 300 miles of the fault and left fences near Point Reyes offset by up to twenty feet. At the northern end the geology changes character: Lassen Peak erupted between 1914 and 1917, the last eruption in the lower 48 states before Mount St. Helens in 1980, and Mount Shasta rises to 14,179 feet as the southern anchor of the Cascade volcanoes. The Sierra Nevada itself is a single granite fault block roughly 400 miles long, lifted and tilted westward, so its eastern escarpment falls more than 10,000 feet into the Owens Valley while the western slope descends gently for a hundred miles.

An entire valley drains through one gap

The Central Valley stretches about 450 miles north to south and 40 to 60 miles across, roughly 20,000 square miles of some of the flattest ground in the country, hemmed in by the Sierra on one side and the Coast Ranges on the other. The Sacramento River flows south down the northern half. The San Joaquin flows north up the southern half. They meet in the Sacramento San Joaquin Delta, push west through Carquinez Strait into San Francisco Bay, and leave through the Golden Gate, the only sea level break in the Coast Ranges for hundreds of miles. Water falling as snow on the Sierra crest has exactly one exit.

That snowpack supplies close to a third of the state's water and is measured every April 1 as the year's most important number. The plumbing built around it is enormous: Shasta Dam at the top of the valley, Oroville Dam at 770 feet as the tallest dam in the country, and the California Aqueduct carrying water 444 miles south. Tulare Lake, once the largest freshwater lake west of the Mississippi, was diverted out of existence by around 1900 for farmland, and briefly reappeared during the wet winter of 2023.

Deserts, and ground far below the sea

Three deserts meet in southeastern California. The Mojave is high desert, mostly between 2,000 and 4,000 feet. The Colorado Desert is the low northwestern lobe of the Sonoran, hot and near sea level. Great Basin sagebrush country runs up the eastern side of the Sierra.

Death Valley holds the extremes. Badwater Basin at 282 feet below sea level is the lowest point in North America, and Furnace Creek recorded 134 degrees on July 10, 1913, still the highest air temperature ever officially logged anywhere on Earth, with 130 degrees repeated in 2020 and 2021. The Salton Sea further south was an accident: between 1905 and 1907 the Colorado River broke through an irrigation intake and poured into the Salton Sink for about eighteen months, leaving a lake whose surface sits roughly 236 feet below sea level and which has been shrinking and growing saltier since. In the Owens Valley, the 233 mile Los Angeles Aqueduct opened in 1913 and ran entirely on gravity. Owens Lake was dry by 1926, and its exposed bed became the single largest source of dust pollution in the United States.

The only large Mediterranean climate in the country

Almost all of California west of the mountains has wet winters and rainless summers, a pattern found on only a small fraction of the planet's land and nowhere else in the United States at this scale. Rain arrives roughly between October and April. San Francisco can go through July and August without measurable precipitation. Everything follows from that: the grasses that cure gold by June, the reservoirs sized for a single wet season, and a fire season that arrives when the offshore Santa Ana and Diablo winds meet vegetation that has not seen rain in months.

The spread within the state is wide. The northwest corner around Gasquet collects more than 80 inches a year, while Death Valley averages about two. Boca, in the Sierra near Truckee, recorded 45 degrees below zero on January 20, 1937. Along the coast, cold upwelling water generates the summer fog bank that keeps a narrow strip damp enough for redwoods to survive the dry season.

Tallest, largest and oldest, all within one state

Hyperion, a coast redwood in Redwood National Park, stands about 380 feet, the tallest known tree on Earth. General Sherman, a giant sequoia on the Sierra's western slope in Sequoia National Park, is the largest tree by volume at roughly 52,500 cubic feet. In the White Mountains east of the Sierra, Great Basin bristlecone pines include an individual over 4,800 years old, the oldest known non clonal tree anywhere. Three separate records, three different elevation bands, one state.

Lake Tahoe, straddling the Nevada line at 1,645 feet deep, is the second deepest lake in the United States after Crater Lake. California holds nine national parks, more than any other state, along with 840 miles of general coastline and nearly ten degrees of latitude between the Mexican border at 32 degrees 32 minutes north and the Oregon line at 42 degrees. That eastern boundary is two straight lines, the 120th meridian coming down from Oregon to the Tahoe area and then a diagonal to the Colorado River, both drawn at the 1849 convention in Monterey before anyone had surveyed the ground they crossed.

California geography questions

What are the highest and lowest points in California?

Mount Whitney at 14,505 feet and Badwater Basin in Death Valley at 282 feet below sea level. They lie about 85 miles apart, both within Inyo County, and they are the highest and lowest points in the contiguous United States.

How fast is the San Andreas Fault moving?

The Pacific plate slides northwest past the North American plate at roughly 46 millimetres, or about two inches, a year along the fault's 800 mile length.

Why does it not rain in California in summer?

California has a Mediterranean climate, with wet winters and dry summers, the only large area of it in the United States. Rain generally falls between October and April, and the long dry season is what drives the fire season.

How long is California's coastline?

About 840 miles of general coastline, third among the states behind Alaska and Florida. Counting bays and inlets, the tidal shoreline is several times longer.

How many national parks does California have?

Nine, more than any other state, including Yosemite, Death Valley, Sequoia, Kings Canyon, Joshua Tree, Redwood, Lassen Volcanic, Channel Islands and Pinnacles.
