WAWashington State Symbols

Capital: Olympia · West · Admitted 1889

Washington's flag is the symbol that sets it apart from every other state, and it does so twice over. It is the only state flag with a green field, and the only one carrying the portrait of a US president. Washington is also the only state named for a president, so the face on the flag and the name on the map are the same man. The nickname comes from the same green: Seattle newspaperman Charles T. Conover proposed "the Evergreen State" around 1890, pointing at the fir and hemlock forests west of the Cascades. It caught on completely and appears on the licence plates, though the legislature never actually voted it into law.

The motto sits slightly apart from the rest. Al-ki is Chinook Jargon, the trading language of the lower Columbia, and it appeared on the territorial seal. It is treated as the state motto by custom rather than by statute, and it does not appear on the modern seal at all.

What is on the Washington state flag

A dark green rectangle, the same green in the fly as in the hoist, with the state seal centred on it: a bust of George Washington in a gold ring, circled by the words "The Seal of the State of Washington 1889." An optional gold fringe runs around the edge. The legislature adopted it in 1923, the last of the 48 states then in the Union to settle on an official flag, and the Daughters of the American Revolution had lobbied for years to get it done. Green was chosen to match the nickname and the forests. Because the seal is printed in full colour on both sides, the Washington flag is one of the most expensive state flags to manufacture, and it is the only one that cannot simply be reversed.

How a jeweller drew the state seal in an afternoon

The seal came out of the 1889 constitutional convention. A committee had prepared an elaborate scene with Mount Rainier, a harbour, a wheat field and grazing sheep. Charles Talcott, an Olympia jeweller, argued it was far too fussy to be engraved or copied cleanly and that the design should be something nobody could get wrong. He drew two concentric circles using an ink bottle and a silver dollar, lettered the state name and the year between them, and pasted a portrait of George Washington cut from a postage stamp into the middle. The convention took his version. Talcott's brothers helped cut the die, and the family was paid a modest fee. In 1967 the state replaced the stamp portrait with one based on Gilbert Stuart's painting, but the layout is still Talcott's.

Symbols that surprise people

  • The state folk song was written by Woody Guthrie. "Roll On, Columbia, Roll On" came out of a month Guthrie spent in 1941 writing songs for the Bonneville Power Administration, and the state adopted it in 1987.
  • There is an official state vegetable, and it is an onion. The Walla Walla sweet onion was designated in 2007.
  • Washington has an official state ship. The Lady Washington, a full-scale replica of an 18th-century brig homeported in Aberdeen, was named in 2007 and has appeared in feature films.
  • The state fossil was pushed through by schoolchildren. Pupils from Windsor Elementary in Cheney campaigned for the Columbian mammoth and got it adopted in 1998.
  • Palouse Falls is the official state waterfall, designated in 2014, a 198-foot drop cut by Ice Age floods in the scablands of eastern Washington.
  • Two shellfish hold official status. The Olympia oyster, the only oyster native to the Northwest coast, was named state oyster in 2014.

Frequently asked questions

What is the state bird of Washington?

The willow goldfinch, a form of the American goldfinch, adopted in 1951 after a vote by Washington schoolchildren.

What is on the Washington state flag?

A dark green field with the state seal in the centre, showing a bust of George Washington in a gold ring inscribed The Seal of the State of Washington 1889. Adopted in 1923, it is the only US state flag with a green field and the only one bearing the portrait of a president.

What is the Washington state motto?

Al-ki, a Chinook Jargon phrase meaning by and by. It appeared on the territorial seal and is used as the state motto by custom rather than by statute.

What is the state flower of Washington?

The coast rhododendron, Rhododendron macrophyllum. Washington women chose it by ballot in 1892 to represent the state at the Chicago World's Fair, and the legislature confirmed it officially in 1959.

Why is Washington called the Evergreen State?

Seattle newspaperman Charles T. Conover proposed the name around 1890 for the fir and hemlock forests west of the Cascades. It is used everywhere, including on licence plates, but was never formally adopted by the legislature.
