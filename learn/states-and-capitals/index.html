There are 50 states in the United States. Each has its own capital city, its own government, and a two-letter postal code. Delaware was the first to ratify the Constitution on December 7, 1787. Hawaii was the last to join, on August 21, 1959.

The full alphabetical list is below, followed by the same 50 states ordered by the date they joined the Union, and a grid of all 50 two-letter abbreviations.

This page lists all 50 United States and their capitals in alphabetical order. Use it as a quick reference, a study aid, or a printable cheat sheet. Each state name links to a dedicated page with detailed facts, history, geography, and a map showing that state's location within the country.

Whether you're studying for a geography test, planning a road trip, or just curious about the 50 states and capitals, this is the most concise list available. The capitals of the 50 US states have been stable for over a century, California's has been Sacramento since 1854, New York's has been Albany since 1797, and so on. Memorising the 50 states and their capitals is a classic part of US education and an enduring quiz favourite.

Capitals that surprise people

Most US state capitals are not the biggest city. This is by design, when states picked their capitals in the 18th and 19th centuries, founders often chose a smaller, more centrally located town to avoid concentrating power in commercial hubs. The result: a long list of capital-vs-largest-city mismatches that trip up almost every American adult quizzed on the spot.

The classic mismatches

  • California: Sacramento (capital) vs Los Angeles (largest city)
  • New York: Albany vs New York City
  • Florida: Tallahassee vs Jacksonville
  • Texas: Austin vs Houston
  • Illinois: Springfield vs Chicago
  • Nevada: Carson City vs Las Vegas
  • Pennsylvania: Harrisburg vs Philadelphia
  • Washington state: Olympia vs Seattle
  • Louisiana: Baton Rouge vs New Orleans
  • Kentucky: Frankfort vs Louisville
  • Missouri: Jefferson City vs Kansas City
  • Alaska: Juneau vs Anchorage (Juneau has no road access)

The capitals that are the biggest city

About 17 of 50: Phoenix (Arizona), Honolulu (Hawaii), Indianapolis (Indiana), Boston (Massachusetts), Atlanta (Georgia), Denver (Colorado), Salt Lake City (Utah), Oklahoma City (Oklahoma), Little Rock (Arkansas), Charleston (West Virginia, though Huntington competes), and a handful of smaller states where the capital is the largest by default (Cheyenne in Wyoming, Boise in Idaho, etc.).

Frequently asked questions

How many US states and capitals are there?

50 states, each with one capital. Washington D.C. is the federal capital but not a state. Puerto Rico, Guam, USVI, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands are US territories with their own capitals (San Juan, Hagåtña, etc.) but they are not states.

What is the smallest state capital?

Montpelier, Vermont, with around 8,000 residents. It is also the only US state capital without a McDonald's.

What is the largest state capital by population?

Phoenix, Arizona, with around 1.6 million residents. It is the 5th largest city in the United States. Other capitals that are also the largest city: Boston, Atlanta, Indianapolis, Denver, Honolulu, Oklahoma City, Salt Lake City.

Which state was admitted to the Union first?

Delaware, on December 7, 1787. The 13 original colonies ratified the Constitution between 1787 and 1790. The most recent state is Hawaii (August 21, 1959), shortly after Alaska (January 3, 1959).

Are state capitals always the largest city?

No, only about 17 of 50 state capitals are also the largest city in their state. The most famous mismatches: California (Sacramento, not Los Angeles), Florida (Tallahassee, not Miami), New York (Albany, not NYC), Illinois (Springfield, not Chicago).
