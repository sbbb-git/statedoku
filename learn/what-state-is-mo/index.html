What state is MO?

MO is Missouri. Jefferson City is the capital, Kansas City is the largest city, and no other state or territory shares the code.

Why the code is MO

Missouri does not get the first two letters of its own name. Those are M and I, and MI went to Michigan, which has the stronger claim because Michigan actually starts with them. So Missouri reached further in and took the O, the fifth letter. That choice was not invented in 1963. Atlases, newspapers and legal filings had been printing Mo. for Missouri since the nineteenth century. The postal code is that old form in capitals with the period removed.

The two-letter system arrived in October 1963, when the Post Office Department published its list of state abbreviations. ZIP Codes had launched that July, and the last line of an address now had to carry a city, a state and five digits without wrapping. Short codes were the fix.

Missouri sat in the hardest part of that list. Eight states begin with M, and only two of them, MA for Massachusetts and MI for Michigan, could take a plain first-two-letters code. The rest borrow a letter from further along: ME and MD take the final letter of Maine and Maryland, MN and MS take the third letter of Minnesota and Mississippi, MT takes the fourth letter of Montana, and MO takes the fifth letter of Missouri.

Montana is the reason MO feels contested. Montana also opens M, O, so on a first-two-letters rule the code fits both states. It went to Missouri, and Montana was pushed to its fourth letter instead.

Quick facts about Missouri

  • Capital: Jefferson City
  • Largest city: Kansas City, ahead of St. Louis
  • Region: Midwest, on the western bank of the Mississippi
  • Statehood: 1821, admitted as the 24th state
  • Population: between 5 and 10 million
  • Time zone: Central
  • Coastline: none, Missouri is landlocked

Where you will see MO

On mail it closes the final address line, as in Jefferson City, MO before the ZIP Code. Vehicle titles and registrations use it too, paired with the state nickname, the Show-Me State.

Sports listings lean on it heavily because Missouri carries four major-league teams in two cities. Broadcasters write Kansas City, MO for the Chiefs and the Royals, and St. Louis, MO for the Cardinals and the Blues.

Two uses of MO have nothing to do with the state. On the New York Stock Exchange, MO is the ticker for Altria Group. In police procedurals and crossword grids, M.O. is short for modus operandi, a method of working. Crossword setters use both senses, so a three-letter clue reading "Show-Me State, briefly" and a clue reading "criminal's method" can produce the same answer on different days.
