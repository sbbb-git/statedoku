Which states does the Colorado River flow through?

Five states on the river, seven in its basin, one compact that shares its water.

The Colorado River runs through or along five states: Colorado, Utah, Arizona, Nevada and California. Two more, Wyoming and New Mexico, are in its basin through tributaries, the Green River from Wyoming and the San Juan and the Gila from New Mexico, which makes seven Colorado River basin states. The river rises in Rocky Mountain National Park in Colorado and flows about 1,450 miles (2,334 km) southwest to Mexico.

How the water is shared: the 1922 compact

On November 24, 1922, the seven states signed the Colorado River Compact in Santa Fe, New Mexico. It splits the basin at Lee Ferry, in northern Arizona, into an Upper Basin and a Lower Basin, and gives each the use of 7.5 million acre-feet of water a year. Colorado, Utah, Wyoming and New Mexico are the Upper Division states; Arizona, Nevada and California the Lower Division states. Some land of Arizona drains to the Upper Basin, and some of New Mexico and Utah to the Lower Basin, but each state belongs to one division.

A treaty signed with Mexico in 1944 promises Mexico 1.5 million acre-feet a year. The compact's shares were set after an unusually wet run of years, and the river has carried less water on average since, which is why the states keep negotiating how to share shortages.

The dams and the two great reservoirs

  • Glen Canyon Dam, near Page, Arizona, completed in 1963, holds back Lake Powell, which reaches far up into Utah.
  • Hoover Dam, in Black Canyon on the Arizona and Nevada line, completed in 1936, holds back Lake Mead, the largest reservoir in the United States when full.
  • Downstream, Davis Dam forms Lake Mohave, Parker Dam forms Lake Havasu, and Imperial Dam sends water into the All-American Canal toward California's Imperial Valley.

The river supplies water to about 40 million people, from Denver and Salt Lake City to Las Vegas, Phoenix, Tucson, Los Angeles and San Diego, and to farms on both sides of the border.

From the Rockies to the Gulf of California

The Colorado begins as a stream in the Never Summer Mountains, in Rocky Mountain National Park. It crosses western Colorado and eastern Utah, where the Green River joins it in Canyonlands National Park, enters Arizona at Lake Powell and cuts the Grand Canyon. Below the canyon it turns south as the state line, first between Arizona and Nevada, then between Arizona and California, and briefly between Arizona and Mexico near Yuma.

Its mouth is on the Gulf of California, in Mexico. In most years since the 1960s, so much of its water is used along the way that the river dries up before it reaches the sea. A planned release in 2014, the pulse flow, let it reach the Gulf again for a short time.

Its main tributaries

  • Green River, its largest tributary, rises in Wyoming and joins the Colorado in Utah.
  • Gunnison River joins at Grand Junction, Colorado.
  • San Juan River flows from Colorado through New Mexico and joins in Lake Powell, in Utah.
  • Little Colorado River joins in the Grand Canyon, in Arizona.
  • Gila River flows from New Mexico across Arizona and joins at Yuma.

Frequently asked questions

How many states does the Colorado River flow through?

Five states are on the river itself: Colorado, Utah, Arizona, Nevada and California. Seven are in its basin, with Wyoming and New Mexico, which drain into it through tributaries: the Green River from Wyoming, the San Juan and the Gila from New Mexico.

What are the seven Colorado River basin states?

Colorado, Utah, Wyoming and New Mexico, the Upper Division states of the 1922 Colorado River Compact, and Arizona, Nevada and California, the Lower Division states.

Where does the Colorado River start and end?

It rises in Rocky Mountain National Park in northern Colorado and flows about 1,450 miles southwest to Mexico. Its mouth is on the Gulf of California, but in most years since the 1960s its water is used up before it gets there.

What is the Colorado River Compact?

An agreement signed by the seven basin states on November 24, 1922. It splits the basin at Lee Ferry, Arizona, and gives the Upper Basin and the Lower Basin 7.5 million acre-feet of water a year each.
