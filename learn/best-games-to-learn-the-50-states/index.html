The best free games to learn the 50 US states

Map drills, typing quizzes, games for kids and daily puzzles, compared on what they teach, what they cost and whether they need an account.

Short answer. To learn where each state sits, drill a click-the-map quiz such as Seterra or Lizard Point. To test your recall against the clock, use Sporcle's typing quiz. For children, Sheppard Software builds up level by level. To keep it up, a daily puzzle takes five minutes. Every free web game below opens in the browser with no sign-up. Most show ads; Seterra and Sporcle sell a plan that removes them.

A note on bias. Statedoku publishes this page and makes some of the games in it. Ours are listed last, with their limits. Facts about the other games were checked on their own sites on October 22, 2026. Prices and features change, so check before paying for anything.

Learn the map

Seterra

Seterra is a long-running map quiz site that has been part of GeoGuessr since 2022. Its USA list holds 110 map quizzes, on the 50 states, abbreviations, capitals, the 13 colonies, territories, rivers, major cities and the counties of each state, plus five picture quizzes on state flags, outlines and the Founding Fathers.

On "The U.S.: 50 States" you either click each named state (Pin) or type the name of the highlighted one (Type). A guided Learn mode is kept for paying accounts. The web version is free with ads, and GeoGuessr Pro, $3.99 a month billed yearly ($6.99 month to month) in October 2026, removes them. The free iOS and Android apps list no ads or in-app purchases.

Best for: building the mental map, then moving on to capitals and counties.

Lizard Point

Lizard Point is a small family-run site from Ontario that has offered free geography quizzes since 1997. Its US states quiz has four modes: Study, Practice (three tries per state, with hints), Test, and Strict test (one try).

Fifteen US quizzes cover the states and capitals (each also split into East and West, and in a typed version), plus major cities, rivers, bodies of water, geophysical regions, tourist spots, flags and airport codes. It is ad-supported and web only. The teacher and student accounts it used to offer were discontinued in March 2023.

Best for: learners who want hints and a gentle ramp rather than a timer.

Mappr US States by Shape

Mappr's shape quiz shows a state outline and asks which state it is, as multiple choice or typed. It adds a speed round, a find-the-state map mode, a learn mode and a daily challenge in which everyone gets the same six shapes.

It is free with no sign-up, and the site says the quiz itself carries no ads.

Best for: recognizing states by shape, the skill silhouette games test.

Test your memory

Sporcle

Sporcle's US States Quiz asks you to type all 50 names in ten minutes. With close to 40 million plays it is a common benchmark, and a Practice mode removes the timer. The US Capitals Quiz works the same way.

Most of Sporcle's catalog is made by its users, so quality varies beyond the main quizzes. Free play shows ads; Sporcle Orange, $3.99 a month or $43.99 a year in October 2026, removes them.

Best for: recall. Typing a name from memory is harder than picking it from a list, which makes it a good test once the map feels familiar.

PurposeGames

PurposeGames hosts quizzes made by its users, among them a 50-question States of the USA Quiz played more than 11 million times. Anyone can play without an account. The site is funded by advertising alone and asks players to allow ads.

Best for: variety, once the basics are in place.

For children

Sheppard Software

Sheppard Software has a US geography track for younger players: a tutorial, then US States levels 1 to 7, US Capitals levels 1 to 3, regions, rivers and lakes, postal abbreviations and a five-mode USA jigsaw. Players identify states, drag them onto the map, or type the first three letters.

It is free and ad-supported, and the newer levels play on tablets and phones.

Best for: children, and anyone who wants to start with drag and drop.

Stack the States

Stack the States and Stack the States 2 by Freecloud Design are paid apps, $2.99 each on iOS and Android. Children answer questions on capitals, shapes, abbreviations, borders, nicknames and flags to win states, then stack them on screen. The iOS listings show no in-app purchases.

Best for: a one-time purchase for a child's tablet.

Our games

Statedoku

Statedoku is this site. The daily puzzle is a 3×3 grid in which each cell needs a state that fits both its row clue and its column clue, such as "Borders Canada" and "Pacific coast". You get five mistakes, and everyone gets the same grid each day.

Next to it, 21 short games cover the map (Place the State, with Easy, Normal and Hard levels), capitals, abbreviations, silhouettes, flags, the 13 colonies, time zones and more, each in English, French and Spanish. There is no account, no ad on the game pages, and a Share to Google Classroom button on each.

Limits: there is no app, and there are fewer specialist quizzes, such as counties or rivers, than on Seterra.

How to learn the states with games

  • Start with the map. Use a click-the-map quiz until you can place every state, one region at a time. Our region-by-region method splits the 50 into four groups.
  • Then recall the names. Switch to a typing quiz: naming a state from memory is a harder test than clicking it.
  • Add capitals, then abbreviations. Our states and capitals list and abbreviations guide give the full lists to check against.
  • Keep it short and regular. Ten minutes a day is easier to keep up than a long session once a week. A daily puzzle gives you a reason to come back.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free game to learn the 50 states?

It depends on the step. To learn where states are, a click-the-map quiz such as Seterra or Lizard Point. To check recall, Sporcle's typing quiz. For young children, Sheppard Software. To keep practicing every day, a daily puzzle such as Statedoku or Statele.

Is Seterra still free?

Yes. Seterra's quizzes are free on the web, with ads. It has been part of GeoGuessr since 2022, and GeoGuessr's paid Pro plan, $3.99 a month billed yearly ($6.99 month to month) in October 2026, removes the ads. The guided Learn mode also needs a paid account.

Do I need an account to play these games?

No, none of the free games on this page asks for one before you play. Seterra and Sporcle ask you to log in only to save your scores.

Which of these games work in a classroom?

All the web games run in a browser without student accounts. Statedoku's games have a Share to Google Classroom button and can be embedded in a class page. Lizard Point discontinued its teacher accounts in March 2023.

Are there games for the state capitals too?

Yes. Seterra and Lizard Point have capitals map quizzes, Sporcle has a US Capitals typing quiz, and Statedoku has State Capitals Match and a capitals typing quiz.
