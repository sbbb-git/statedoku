The US flag: why 50 stars and 13 stripes

One star per state, one stripe per original colony, 27 versions since 1777.

The flag of the United States has 50 white stars on a blue field, one for each state, and 13 horizontal stripes, 7 red and 6 white, one for each of the original 13 colonies. The 50-star version has been the official flag since July 4, 1960, after Hawaii became the 50th state. It is the 27th version of the flag: the number of stars grew as new states joined, and the stripes have stayed at 13 since 1818.

Why 13 stripes and 50 stars

On June 14, 1777, the Second Continental Congress resolved that the flag of the United States be 13 stripes, alternate red and white, and that the union be 13 stars, white in a blue field, "representing a new constellation". June 14 is Flag Day for that reason.

When Vermont and Kentucky joined, the Flag Act of 1794 gave the flag 15 stars and 15 stripes from May 1, 1795. That is the flag that flew over Fort McHenry in 1814 and inspired "The Star-Spangled Banner". Adding a stripe for every state could not last, so the Flag Act of April 4, 1818 went back to 13 stripes for good and set the rule still in the US Code today: one star for each state, and a new star added on the Fourth of July after a state is admitted.

The 27 versions of the flag

Each row is a flag that became official. States admitted between two Fourths of July shared one new flag; five states admitted between 1796 and 1817 waited for the 1818 law to get their stars.

The 48-star flag flew for 47 years, from 1912 to 1959. The 50-star flag passed that mark on July 4, 2007, and has been in use longer than any other version since.

Proportions and layout

The flag's design is fixed by Executive Order 10834, signed by President Eisenhower on August 21, 1959, the day Hawaii was admitted. The flag is 1.9 times as wide as it is high, the blue union covers the top seven stripes, and the 50 stars sit in nine rows that alternate six and five stars.

What the colors mean

No law gives the colors a meaning. The explanation most often quoted is Charles Thomson's, written in 1782 for the same three colors on the Great Seal of the United States: white for purity and innocence, red for hardiness and valor, and blue for vigilance, perseverance and justice.

Frequently asked questions

How many stars and stripes are on the US flag?

Fifty stars, one for each state, and 13 stripes, 7 red and 6 white, one for each of the original 13 colonies.

When did the US flag get 50 stars?

On July 4, 1960, the first Fourth of July after Hawaii was admitted as the 50th state on August 21, 1959.

How many versions of the US flag have there been?

27, from the 13-star flag of 1777 to the 50-star flag of 1960. The number of stars changed each time; the 13 stripes have been the rule since 1818.

When is a new star added to the flag?

Under the Flag Act of 1818, still in the US Code (4 U.S.C. section 2), a new star is added on the Fourth of July after a state is admitted.
