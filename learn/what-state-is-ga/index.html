What state is GA?

GA is Georgia, the US state. Atlanta is both the capital and the largest city. The country also called Georgia is a separate place and uses GE.

Why the code is GA

Georgia does not begin with G and A. It begins with G and E. The code is the first letter of the name joined to the last, the same construction used for ME in Maine and MD in Maryland. It also preserves Ga., the form that atlases, gazetteers and newspaper datelines had used for well over a century before the postal service picked it up.

Georgia had a free hand that most states did not. It is the only state whose name starts with G, so no rival needed a distinguishing letter and no compromise was forced. Contrast that with Missouri and Montana, which both open M and O and had to be split into MO and MT, or with the four New states, which had to be told apart by their second word.

When the Post Office Department published its list of two-letter abbreviations in October 1963, the goal was narrow: ZIP Codes had gone live that summer, and a city, a state and five digits had to fit on one address line. The list covered US mail and nothing else. It was never designed to sit alongside international country codes, and the Republic of Georgia was assigned GE under that separate system. One name, two standards, two letters that do not match. That gap is the reason the question gets typed into search bars at all.

Quick facts about Georgia

  • Capital: Atlanta
  • Largest city: Atlanta, one of the minority of states where capital and largest city are the same
  • Region: South, Deep South, Eastern time
  • Statehood: 1788, the fourth state to ratify the Constitution
  • Colonial rank: the last of the thirteen colonies to be founded, in 1733
  • Population: over 10 million
  • Nickname: the Peach State

Where you will see GA

Mail is the origin: Atlanta, GA sits ahead of the ZIP Code on the last address line, and the same code runs through vehicle titles, registrations and tax filings.

Sports coverage uses it constantly because Atlanta carries four professional franchises. Datelines read Atlanta, GA for the Braves in baseball, the Falcons in football, the Hawks in basketball and Atlanta United in soccer. College coverage adds a wrinkle of its own, since UGA stands for the University of Georgia while GA on its own still means the state.

Forms are where the state-versus-country problem bites hardest. Any shipping or billing form that asks separately for a country and a state can accept Georgia in either field, and picking the wrong one sends a parcel to the wrong hemisphere. Crossword grids play the ambiguity for fun instead, cluing GA through the Peach State one week and through gallium the next.
