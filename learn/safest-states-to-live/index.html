Safest states to live in the US

Maine, New Hampshire, Connecticut and Rhode Island lead on FBI 2024 figures. Crime rankings by violent and property crime, and the most dangerous states.

Crime in the United States is highly geographic. The same FBI data that puts Maine at about one violent crime per 1,000 residents puts Alaska at about one per 140. This guide compares the 50 states on the two standard FBI Uniform Crime Reporting measures, violent crime rate and property crime rate, both per 100,000 residents, for 2024.

Why Maine ranks #1

Maine recorded 100.1 violent crimes per 100,000 residents in 2024, under a third of the national rate of 359.1. Its aggravated assault rate, 55.0, was the lowest of the 50 states and its robbery rate, 11.0, the fourth lowest. The FBI figures do not say why, but Maine has two traits that go with lower violent crime:

  • No large city. About 1.4 million people live in the state, and Portland, the largest city, had about 68,000 residents at the 2020 census.
  • An older population. Maine has the highest median age of the 50 states, and violent crime is concentrated among young adults.

New Hampshire, second, had the lowest homicide rate (1.0 per 100,000) and the lowest burglary rate (48.1) of the 50 states in 2024. Its homicide rate is about a tenth of Louisiana's (10.8) or New Mexico's (10.5).

What the rankings hide

State averages can be misleading. A few important caveats:

  • One city can move a state. Memphis distorts Tennessee, New Orleans distorts Louisiana, Albuquerque distorts New Mexico.
  • Urban vs rural. Rural counties tend to have lower violent crime than big cities, even inside "dangerous" states.
  • Reporting differences. Some agencies stopped reporting under the FBI's new NIBRS system, leaving gaps in coverage.
  • Property crime patterns shift. Auto theft has spiked in cities like Denver, Memphis and St. Louis in recent years.
  • Property > violent. In raw numbers, property crime dwarfs violent crime everywhere, but it weighs less heavily on quality of life.

Safest regions of the US

Grouped by our 4 census regions:

  • Northeast, the safest region on both measures in 2024 (violent 279.9, property 1,462.0), led by New England (ME, NH, CT, RI).
  • Midwest, mixed: Nebraska and Iowa are in the lowest third for violent crime; Missouri and Kansas are among the ten highest.
  • West, the highest-crime region on both measures in 2024 (violent 434.5, property 2,086.0), though Wyoming ranks fifth and Utah and Idaho sit in the lowest third for violent crime; Alaska, New Mexico, California and Colorado are among the most dangerous.
  • South, second for violent crime (364.3) and the highest homicide rate of the four regions (6.2 per 100,000).

Beyond crime: other safety factors

If you're using "safest" in a broader sense, weigh these too:

  • Natural disasters. Hurricanes (FL, LA, TX coast), tornadoes (OK, KS, MO, AL), earthquakes (CA, AK), wildfires (CA, OR, WA), floods (LA, MS), see our guide to states with the most natural disasters.
  • Road safety. Mississippi, South Carolina and Wyoming have the highest road fatality rates per capita; Massachusetts and New York the lowest.
  • Healthcare access. Massachusetts and Hawaii consistently rank highest; Mississippi and Texas lowest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the safest state?

Maine has had the lowest violent crime rate in recent FBI data, with New Hampshire close behind, followed by Connecticut and Rhode Island.

What is the most dangerous state?

Alaska by violent crime rate per capita. New Mexico is often #2.

Why is the South higher in crime?

By homicide, yes: in 2024 the South had 6.2 per 100,000, the highest of the four census regions, against 5.0 nationally. By overall violent and property crime, the West was higher. Higher poverty rates are the factor most often cited for the South's homicide rate.

Where can I check official numbers?

The FBI's Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) and the newer National Incident-Based Reporting System (NIBRS) publish state-level data annually.

Is New York City really safer than the US average?

By homicide rate, yes: NYC has been below the US average for years and is one of the safest large US cities, though its overall violent crime rate is above the national average.
