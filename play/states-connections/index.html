States Connections

NYT-Connections style: group 16 US states into 4 hidden categories.

Categories0/4 Mistakes0/4
Find the 4 groups of 4 states
Pick 4 states that share a category
 

Sixteen states, four hidden categories, four states in each. Unlike a capitals quiz this one tests classification rather than recall: you have to know which states border Canada, which levy no income tax, which seceded, which sit in the Rust Belt. One board takes three to five minutes.

How to play

Press Start and a grid of sixteen state names appears in random order. Select exactly four you think share a category, then press Submit. A correct group locks in, turns a solid color, and its category label is revealed above the grid. A wrong group clears your selection and costs one of four mistakes. Four mistakes ends the board. Your score is groups solved out of four, so solving all four is three stars, three groups is two stars.

There is no timer and no penalty for deselecting, so nothing is lost by rearranging tiles before you commit.

Solve the closed lists first

The categories in play are drawn from a fixed set of national groupings, and some of those groupings are tiny. That is your opening move: find the category with the fewest members in the whole country, because on a board of sixteen it has the least room to hide.

Where boards trip you up

Many states genuinely belong to more than one grouping. Texas borders Mexico, has no income tax, was Confederate and sits in the Sun Belt. Florida is Sun Belt, Bible Belt, Confederate, no income tax and Spanish heritage all at once. So a tile that fits your hunch is weak evidence. The strong move is to find the state that fits only one category on the board and build outward from it.

Two specific edges catch people. Border Canada is not just the northern tier you picture: Ohio and Pennsylvania both count through their Great Lakes water boundary, while Idaho, Montana, North Dakota, Minnesota, Michigan, New York, Vermont, New Hampshire, Maine, Washington and Alaska make up the rest. And landlocked here means no ocean and no Great Lakes frontage, which is why Michigan, Illinois and Ohio sit outside the group of twenty even though none of them touches salt water.
