How to ace State Abbreviations

Where the USPS codes come from, the 8 M-state confusion, and the AL/AK pair that breaks the pattern.

Memorizing all 50 USPS two-letter state abbreviations is hard for one reason: too many states share starting letters. There are 8 M-states, 8 N-states and 4 A-states. This guide walks through how the codes came about in 1963, the confused pairs, and a memorization order that builds on patterns instead of brute force.

Where the codes come from (1963)

The Post Office Department, the predecessor of today's USPS, published the two-letter codes in October 1963 in Publication 59: Abbreviations for Use with ZIP Code, a few months after ZIP codes were introduced. The aim was practical: fit the city, the state and the ZIP code on one line of no more than 23 characters.

  • Always two letters, uppercase, no periods. "CA", not "Ca." or "Calif."
  • One code changed later. Nebraska was first NB and became NE in November 1969, to avoid confusion with New Brunswick in Canada.
  • No clash with Canada. The state codes do not overlap with the Canadian province and territory codes.

The 8 M-states: the biggest source of errors

Eight states begin with M. Their codes are not in alphabetical order, and only two of them use the second letter of the name.

The M-state memory trick

Split the 8 M-states into two groups:

  • Group A, the Ma- and Me- states: Massachusetts MA, Maryland MD, Maine ME. MA uses the second letter; MD and ME take the D of Maryland and the final E of Maine.
  • Group B, the Mi- and Mo- states: Michigan MI, Minnesota MN, Mississippi MS, Missouri MO, Montana MT. Only MI uses the second letter; the others take a later letter.

The A-states: AL, AK, AZ, AR

Four states start with A, and they form the second-most-confused cluster:

  • Alabama, AL, second letter L, the obvious choice.
  • Alaska, AK, the K of Alaska.
  • Arizona, AZ, the Z of Arizona. Easy to remember: no other state name contains a Z.
  • Arkansas, AR, second letter R, mirroring the "Ar-" start.

Memory trick: Alabama and Arkansas keep their first two letters, Alaska and Arizona take a later letter.

The 8 N-states

Eight states start with N, as many as start with M:

  • Nebraska, NE
  • Nevada, NV
  • New Hampshire, NH
  • New Jersey, NJ
  • New Mexico, NM
  • New York, NY
  • North Carolina, NC
  • North Dakota, ND

The "New" states (NH, NJ, NM, NY) use N + first letter of the second word, easy. The "North" states use N + first letter of "Carolina" or "Dakota". Nebraska and Nevada break the pattern: NE and NV.

Nebraska vs Nevada, the easiest swap

Nebraska is NE. Nevada is NV. Mnemonic: Nevada has Las Vegas, V for Vegas. Nebraska was NB until 1969.

The C-states and the K-trick

  • California, CA
  • Colorado, CO
  • Connecticut, CT
  • Kansas, KS
  • Kentucky, KY

Kansas and Kentucky are the only two states that start with K, and both take a later letter: the S of Kansas and the Y of Kentucky. The three C-states take the second letter (CA, CO) or a later one (CT).

The Carolina/Dakota/Virginia pairs

PairNorth codeSouth / West code
North/South CarolinaNCSC
North/South DakotaNDSD
Virginia / West VirginiaVAWV

The compass-direction states always start the code with N, S, or W. Virginia gets the prime "VA", West Virginia uses "WV".

Codes that don't match the state name at all

A few codes feel arbitrary. Memorize them as exceptions:

  • Iowa, IA (not IO)
  • Louisiana, LA (not the city of Los Angeles)
  • Hawaii, HI (sounds friendly: "Hi!")
  • Pennsylvania, PA (not PE or PN)
  • Texas, TX (not TE, the X is distinctive)

Frequently asked questions

Are there older state abbreviations than the 1963 postal codes?

Yes. Before 1963, abbreviations were longer (e.g., "Calif.", "Mass.", "Penn.") and varied between government agencies. The 1963 codes standardized everything into 2 letters, all caps, no periods, for ZIP code automation.

Can I use AE, AP, or AA as state codes?

Those are not states, they are USPS codes for U.S. military addresses: AE (Armed Forces Europe), AP (Armed Forces Pacific), and AA (Armed Forces Americas). They appear in mail addresses but never on official state lists.

Why does Washington State get WA and Washington D.C. get DC?

Washington State (admitted 1889) got WA. The District of Columbia (the federal capital district) gets DC because it isn't a state, it's a separate federal jurisdiction.

Which state abbreviations are easiest to confuse?

The MI / MN / MO / MS cluster is the worst. AL / AK is a close second. MA / ME / MD trips up anyone who learns by alphabet.
