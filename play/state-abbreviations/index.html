This quiz drills one narrow skill: seeing a two letter USPS code and naming the state behind it without stopping to think. Twenty codes per game, sampled fresh from all fifty, so a full round runs about ninety seconds.

How to play

A code fills the navy card. Four state names sit underneath it. Tap the one that matches.

  • Twenty rounds per game, drawn without repeats from the fifty states, so no two games hand you the same twenty codes.
  • One point per correct answer, added the moment you tap.
  • Three lives. A wrong tap turns your choice red, turns the correct name green, and costs a heart.
  • Lose all three hearts and the game ends early, before round twenty.
  • Stars follow your percentage: 18 out of 20 earns three stars, 12 earns two, 6 earns one.
  • Your best score and your day to day streak live in your own browser. No account, no sign in.

The eight M states decide most games

Eight states begin with M and their codes are not in alphabetical order. Because the three wrong choices are drawn at random from the other 49 states, an M code often lands next to a second M state, and that is where the hearts go.

Cut the eight in half. MA, MD, ME and MI take a letter from the front of the name. MN, MO, MS and MT reach further in for a consonant nobody else claims. Three other clusters cost almost as much. Alabama holds AL while Alaska, admitted 140 years later in 1959, takes AK. Nebraska is NE and Nevada is NV, so hang the V on Las Vegas. Arizona is AZ and Arkansas is AR, the only pair where a single rare letter does all the work.

The N group splits cleanly. NH, NJ, NM and NY take the first letter of the second word. NC and ND take the first letter of Carolina and Dakota. Only Nebraska and Nevada break the pattern, which is exactly why they are the pair people miss.
