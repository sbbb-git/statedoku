Grid redesign on the Metrodoku model, constraint explanations, asset version fixes

THE GRID

The grid was drawn as a table: one shared 1px gap over a border-coloured
background, cells with no outline of their own, constraint labels carrying
gradients and a single accent edge. Everything read as one dense block.

It is now a set of separated cards. gap goes from 1px to 10px on desktop and
6px on phones, each cell gets its own 2px navy outline and a 10px radius, and
the constraint labels become flat tinted cards in the same shape as the cells
they rule over. Filled and wrong states now colour their own border rather
than sitting under a gradient, so the state of a cell is readable from its
edge instead of from a wash. The empty-cell star is replaced by a faint plus:
the star decorated, the plus says the cell is waiting for input.

The wrapper loses its card chrome, since the cards are the design now. On
phones it also loses the negative side margins that used to bleed the grid to
the screen edge, which would have clipped the new outlines.

CONSTRAINT EXPLANATIONS

Opaque labels were the loudest complaint about the puzzle, and "Medical drama
setting" is not something a player can reason their way to. Constraints now
carry a plain-language gloss, shown in a popover anchored under the label.

105 of the 156 constraints the generator can pick are explained, across all
three languages. The other 51 are things like "Starts with M" that explain
themselves, and they draw no affordance at all: the "?" only appears where
there is something worth saying, so it means something when it shows up. The
whole card is the tap target rather than the small glyph, which is what makes
it usable on a phone. Escape, an outside tap, or a second tap closes it.

The glosses deliberately never name the states that satisfy a constraint, so
none of them leak an answer. Where a label is genuinely ambiguous they say so
outright: "Top 5 largest by area" now adds that this is about size, not
population, and "Admitted before 1800" that this is the date of statehood,
not of settlement.

ASSET VERSIONS

The cache-buster versions had drifted apart: style.css was pinned at v=17 on
973 pages, v=18 on 275 and v=19 on 755, for one single file. Worse, the French
and Spanish homepages still asked for game.js?v=11 while English was on v=14,
so a returning FR or ES player kept the build from before the puzzle fix and
went on being told their correct answers were wrong. All 2,011 pages now agree
on one version per asset.

The JSON under /data/ was fetched with no version query at all, so translations
and state data could never reach a returning player once cached. Both fetches
now carry CONFIG.DATA_VERSION.

VOICE

The earlier em-dash sweep covered HTML and JSON but never looked at the JS, so
14 user-visible strings still carried them: cell aria-labels, the "already on
the board" announcement, the share text that goes out with every result, the
archive date line, and the placeholders in the stats panel. Plus 23 range
dashes in constraint labels. Zero unicode dashes remain in any user-facing
string; what is left lives in code comments.

NOTES

Rewrote the three how-to-play pages to describe the rules as they now work
rather than as they used to, around 800 words each.

Audited the constraint pool for redundancy: 16 groups match exactly the same
states, mostly a built-in constraint and its pop-culture twin (tz_eastern and
pc_eastern_time, contains_letter_k and pc_contains_k, and so on). Generating a
full year found 0 grids that pair two of them, so nothing is broken today and
nothing is changed here. Removing the duplicates would shift the pool the
seeded RNG indexes into and rewrite every archived puzzle, which is not worth
it for a fault that never fires.

Verified in a browser at 1280 and 375 wide, in all three languages: cards and
outlines render, the popover anchors under its label and stays inside the
viewport on a phone, the picker still opens below the grid on desktop and as a
sheet on mobile, and two states that both satisfy their clues are both
accepted and both survive a reload.
